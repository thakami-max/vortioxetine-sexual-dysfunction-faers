# Supplementary File S15. Time-window and calendar-period sensitivity analyses for potential launch-period or Weber-type reporting dynamics

## Purpose
This supplementary file reports additional time-window sensitivity analyses conducted to evaluate whether the primary vortioxetine-versus-escitalopram finding was driven by early post-launch reporting dynamics for vortioxetine.

## Design
The analysis used the processed primary V2 cohort and restricted reports by **FDA_DT**, the FDA receipt date used in the case-versioning and deduplication logic. The main time-restricted analysis excluded 2014 Q1–2016 Q4 and retained reports with **FDA_DT in 2017 Q1 or later**. All other primary-analysis restrictions were retained: primary-suspect (PS) drug role, depression-related indication restriction, monotherapy-like restriction, and the narrow sexual-dysfunction preferred-term dictionary.

## Time-restricted cohort, 2017 Q1 onward
- Final exposure cases: 44,059
- Vortioxetine reports: 4,594; sexual-dysfunction cases: 158
- Escitalopram reports: 4,107; sexual-dysfunction cases: 264
- Paroxetine reports: 2,611; sexual-dysfunction cases: 61
- Sertraline reports: 9,079; sexual-dysfunction cases: 440
- Duloxetine reports: 3,099; sexual-dysfunction cases: 54
- Venlafaxine reports: 6,669; sexual-dysfunction cases: 191
- Bupropion reports: 4,608; sexual-dysfunction cases: 56
- Mirtazapine reports: 2,612; sexual-dysfunction cases: 34

## Primary time-restricted result
In the 2017 Q1 onward subset, vortioxetine remained associated with lower sexual-dysfunction reporting than escitalopram: **158/4,594 vs 264/4,107; ROR 0.52, 95% CI 0.42–0.63**.

## Pairwise time-restricted results
Vortioxetine showed lower reporting than escitalopram, sertraline, and pooled SSRIs; no clear difference versus venlafaxine; and higher reporting than paroxetine, duloxetine, bupropion, mirtazapine, and pooled SNRIs. Detailed 2 × 2 counts and estimates are provided in S15a and S15b.

## Sex-stratified primary comparison
The time-restricted vortioxetine-versus-escitalopram comparison remained lower in both male reports (**ROR 0.52, 95% CI 0.40–0.68**) and female reports (**ROR 0.67, 95% CI 0.47–0.95**). Detailed counts are provided in S15c.

## Calendar-period analysis
In calendar-period analyses, the vortioxetine-versus-escitalopram estimate was attenuated and crossed the null in 2014 Q1–2017 Q4 (ROR 0.79, 95% CI 0.53–1.17), but was consistent in 2018 Q1–2021 Q4 (ROR 0.53, 95% CI 0.38–0.72) and 2022 Q1–2025 Q4 (ROR 0.53, 95% CI 0.40–0.72). Detailed counts are provided in S15d.

## Interpretation
These analyses suggest that the principal vortioxetine-versus-escitalopram finding was not driven by early post-launch reporting dynamics. Residual temporal reporting bias cannot be fully excluded in spontaneous-reporting data.
